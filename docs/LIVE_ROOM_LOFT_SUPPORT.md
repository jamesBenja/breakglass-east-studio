## 2. Live room proposal: the machinery stays, music moves around it
**Organising idea:**
- The **high tray zone** (4.33 m, about 90 m²) is the music room.
- The **low under-loft zone** (2.59 m) is the machinery's home and the drier, more intimate recording space.
- A painted **3.7 m machinery aisle** on the building axis runs from the garage door through to the under-loft bays and stays clear of permanent fixtures.

| Mode | Layout (in the model) |
|---|---|
| **Farm mode** | Machines drive or tow in on the aisle and park in the two marked **storage envelopes under the loft** (each about 4 × 5.5 m). The piano is rolled to the tray zone's west side on a locked caster dolly under a padded, humidity-buffering cover. Gobos are nested against the side walls. |
| **Grand piano + solo** | The piano sits in the tray zone under the 4.33 m flat ceiling, angled about 25° off-axis to the long walls (avoiding parallel-wall flutter paths), lid toward the open room. Variable panels are set reflective. |
| **String quartet / chamber (4–12)** | Arc in the tray zone (shown: a quartet arc of radius 1.2 m beside the piano). The overlook gives a natural high room-mic position. |
| **Small orchestra (about 15–20 players)** | The whole tray zone plus the aisle (about 90 m² at roughly 4–5 m² per player with spacing). The machines must be parked under the loft, and the under-loft zone is screened with gobos. **Twenty-plus players is a practical ceiling here**, not a full orchestra. |
| **Band / production** | Drums under the loft (lower, drier) with gobos; amps in the middle-zone iso room; vocals in the iso room. |

**Rules for coexisting with machinery** (rules, not design opinions; owner to formalise as a house rule):
- No machine is run during sessions.
- Machines enter and leave only in farm mode, with the garage door open and the instruments covered.
- Fuel tanks and cans are stored outside or in a ventilated, fire-rated cabinet.
- Drip trays go under each machine.
- A CO alarm is installed, and a purge fan runs after any movement.
- The piano gets a humidity-control system plus a cover.
- The actual machinery list (dimensions, turning paths) is **needed from the owner**. The red blocks in the renders are honestly labelled **placeholders**, not models of real machines.

**Acoustics:**
- Hinged **variable-acoustic panels** between the windows on both long walls of the tray zone: one face reclaimed wood, the other absorptive.
- Movable gobos (Clark wood face plus absorber).
- The concrete floor stays (rugged, honest). Rugs are added per session.
- The under-loft zone's foil insulation should be covered (for fire, and to stop HF glare) with timber battens over mineral wool. This is the most important acoustic change in that zone.
- Isolation from the garage door: the existing overhead door is a weak acoustic barrier. Option: an inner insulated sliding acoustic door on the same opening, so no new partition is needed.

**Infrastructure:**
- Floor boxes for mic tie-lines at the tray-zone perimeter.
- A cable trough along the corridor to the control room.
- Video cameras for line-of-sight to the control room, since there is no window between the rooms.
- Cue and talkback.
- Industrial pendants plus a dimmable high-CRI wash for filming.

## 3. Middle zone: support rooms inside the existing partitions (no new walls)
| Room | Proposed use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| East room nearest the control room (about 4.7 × 2.0 m) | **Machine room** (computers, converters, amplifiers, UPS) | Next to the control room for short cable runs; noise kept out of both studios |
| West room nearest the control room (about 5.2 × 4.5 m) | **Vocal / overdub iso booth** | Largest room, quiet, near the control room |
| Rooms nearest the live room | **Amp / iso booth** and **storage** (mic locker, cases, gobo parking) | Short runs to the live room; equipment staging off the corridor |
| Corridor | Circulation + cable trough + coat and shoe zone (farm/studio threshold) | A mud zone matters in a working farm building |

All require: door and seal upgrades, quiet ventilation, and finishes. **No partitions are added or removed.** The existing partitions, door locations and headroom (2.54 m) are scan-inferred and need tape confirmation.

## 4. Loft: preserve the option, invest nothing irreversible
- **Now:**
  - Overlook guard to code height (architect).
  - Use as a **room-mic / ambience position** and light storage.
  - Keep a future stair location free, and run one spare conduit.
- **Bedroom: probably not viable as built.** Headroom ≥ 2.1 m covers only about a 2 m-wide strip under a 13° roof, and the roof may not be raised. The joists were framed for an attic; a bedroom needs a code stair, egress windows, fire separation from a machinery room, and structural reinforcement.
- **Recommendation:** treat the bedroom as a **feasibility question for the architect and structural engineer**, not a design item. If accommodation is wanted, a separate building or an existing house on the property is likely to be cheaper and safer.
